The pipeline

From raw trades to a scored alert

Every new coin goes through the same screening pipeline. Each stage can reject it — most launches never reach Telegram, which is the point.

  1. 01

    Watch new Solana launches

    Continuously picks up fresh Solana memecoins as they appear, so you don't have to sit on a new-pairs page. Read-only; the bot never signs or sends a transaction.

    scan every 15s
  2. 02

    Check the contract

    Mint and freeze authority must be renounced. Honeypot patterns and wash-trading flags are rejected on sight. If contract data is missing, the coin is skipped rather than guessed at.

    fail-closed
  3. 03

    Inspect who holds it

    Top-10 concentration, insider and dev holdings, bundled buys, snipers, and fresh-wallet ratios are measured. Supply that looks stacked against late buyers is filtered out.

    holder & bundle checks
  4. 04

    Check the deployer

    Looks at the dev's launch history and rug ratio. Serial launchers and wallets with a record of abandoned tokens don't make it through.

    dev history
  5. 05

    Check the market

    Minimum market cap, liquidity, holders, and token age, plus checks for tokens still on the bonding curve. Coins too thin or too young to assess are held back.

    market gates
  6. 06

    Score and deliver

    Survivors get a 0–100 safety score. Coins at or above the pass mark arrive in Telegram as a compact card with the contract address, key stats, and security flags. Each token alerts once.

    Telegram group

Why it helps

Built to reject more than it sends

New-coin feeds are mostly rugs, bundles, and wash volume. Chainseer does the first pass for you and throws almost all of it away before it reaches your phone.

⛨

Anti-rug screening, on by default

Renounced authorities, honeypot checks, wash-trading rejection, insider and dev-hold caps, sniper counts, bundler rate, and bonding-curve exclusion all run on every coin. The goal is simple: filter almost all rugs so you don't have to inspect them by hand.

◷

Less time scrolling

Instead of opening every new launch, you get a short list of coins that already cleared the basics.

⇄

Fail-closed

If data for a check is missing or unclear, the coin is skipped. Chainseer prefers to miss a coin than to pass a risky one.

★

Deployer awareness

Dev launch history and rug ratio are checked, so repeat offenders are screened out before you see them.

◎

One alert per token

No re-alert spam when a coin chops sideways. The alert-once set is persisted in SQLite, so a restart cannot replay old calls.

⟲

Outcome tracking keeps the filter honest

Every alert is recorded with its entry price. Checkpoints at roughly 15 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours, and 24 hours feed a final review, including whether a coin later collapsed by 70% or more. That shows how well the filters are really working.

Questions

Before you join

Does Chainseer trade for me?

No. The bot only reads public market and on-chain data and never connects to a wallet, holds funds, signs transactions, or places orders. Every decision after an alert is yours.

Is this financial advice?

No. Chainseer publishes informational signals about on-chain activity. It does not know your circumstances, it cannot predict price, and a high score is not a recommendation to buy.

Which chain and data sources?

Solana only. Chainseer reads public token, holder, and security data from third-party market data providers and on-chain sources. It is an independent project and is not affiliated with Telegram or Solana.

How fast is an alert?

New coins are scanned every 15 seconds. Delivery depends on data-provider response time, rate limits, and Telegram — so treat timing as best-effort, not guaranteed.

Why do some tokens alert only once?

Alert-once is enabled by default and persisted in the database. It prevents repeat calls on the same token, even across bot restarts. Operators can change the cooldown behaviour through configuration.

Does a passing coin mean it's safe?

No. Chainseer filters out almost all rugs, but no filter catches everything. A pass means a coin cleared the checks, not that it is safe or will go up. Treat each alert as a shortlist and do your own research.

What does the score mean?

It is a 0–100 ranking of how cleanly a coin cleared the contract, holder, market, and deployer checks. It is a filter, not a probability of profit.

Do I need a wallet or an API key?

No. Followers only need Telegram. Any data-provider API keys are configured by the operator running the bot.
